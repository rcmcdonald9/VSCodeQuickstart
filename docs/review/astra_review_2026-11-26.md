The main objection is not that the motors are obviously too small. **Five assumptions most likely to be wrong**

1. “Assemblable shaft” means an installable transmission with positive torque and axial retention all the way to the tool.
2. A nominal KX spring preload and room-temperature contact model predict hot, repeatable slip and safe retention in printed parts.
3. A taped 7.8 mm bundle has the zero-thickness spiral's travel, current capacity and camera signal integrity over repeated full-range motion.
4. A gravity model removes 80% of deformation across payloads, directions and temperatures, leaving joint encoder RSS as the dominant error.
5. The catalogue rated torque, nominal balancer law and 0.6 kg task-day thermal result all apply simultaneously to a long 1 kg hold in the built enclosure.

**Five strongest aspects**

1. Sampling bent and rolled poses exposed **upper-yaw loading** that a straight-arm calculation misses; retaining the GIM there is well motivated.
2. **Straddle-supported metal small pulleys** and separate link bearings are a sound way to keep belt pull off motor output bearings once the interfaces are completed.
3. The **P1/P2 comparison and seat-rotation-to-tool calculation** found a consequential error and identified the elbow housing as the best stiffness target.
4. The design explicitly recognizes **regeneration, non-sinking supplies, independent logic and the need for hardware torque removal** rather than assigning emergency stopping to the policy.
5. A **coupon → single axis → bench arm** progression, paired with joint-side sensing and an already functioning closed-loop software evaluation, can retire the expensive uncertainties before a full-robot build.
