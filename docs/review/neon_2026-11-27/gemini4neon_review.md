### Design Review Findings

1. **Safety board netlist has three circuit-level bugs: destructive inductive flyback on the clamp, contactor chatter on bus sag, and an unblowable main fuse**
   * **Severity:** Critical
   * **Evidence:** `docs/rnd/electrical.md` (*A5 v2: Safety board*, *Bench supply*, *Regen clamp*). `R1–R3` are **3 × Vishay RH-50 0.56 Ω** (wirewound) switched at **49 A each (146 A total)** by `Q1–Q3` (`IRFB7530`, 60 V) via a fast `UCC27524` gate driver and `TLV3702` comparator (0.4 V hysteresis) with no flyback diodes. `Q4` (K1 coil) is gated by `LATCH ∧ WD_OK ∧ PRE_OK` (`PRE_OK`: `VBUS ≥ VIN − 3 V`). `U6` (`SN74LVC1G123`) triggers on `B = AUX_OK falling edge`. Main fuse `F1` is **40 A ATO** on the `SE-1000-24` (**41.7 A** CC limit).
   * **Why it matters physically:**
     * *Inductive flyback & oscillation:* Standard Vishay **RH-50** resistors are inductive wirewound (the non-inductive part is **NH-50**), with ~2–5 µH plus lead inductance. Switching 49 A off in ~100 ns produces $V = L\frac{di}{dt} > 500\text{ V}$, immediately avalanching and shorting the 60 V `IRFB7530`s. Furthermore, on the 7.1 mF bench bus (11.8 mF robot), 146 A drops `VBUS` by 0.4 V in **19 µs** (32 µs robot), faster than the nanopower `TLV3702`'s ~36–60 µs propagation delay—causing severe undershoot and ~10 kHz hard-switched banging.
     * *Contactor chatter:* Because `PRE_OK` stays in K1's hold equation after K1 closes, any transient bus sag > 3 V (drive surge or supply current-limit foldback) opens K1 while `LATCH` stays high, immediately re-closing K2 (`LATCH ∧ ¬AUX`) and cycling K1/K2 until contacts weld or `R3` burns.
     * *Pinout & fuse errors:* On `SN74LVC1G123`, pin `B` is rising-edge triggered (`Ā` is falling-edge). A 40 A ATO fuse carries 104% rated current (41.7 A) indefinitely and will never clear a fault fed by the SE-1000-24.
   * **Concrete fix or test:** Replace `RH-50` with **NH-50** non-inductive resistors and add a fast 60 V / 60 A freewheeling Schottky across each resistor; replace `TLV3702` with a fast comparator (e.g. `TLV3202`, < 100 ns) and widen clamp hysteresis to ~0.8 V or add a minimum-on-time monostable; latch K1's `PRE_OK` condition with `AUX` (`Q4 = LATCH ∧ WD_OK ∧ (PRE_OK ∨ AUX)`) and clear `LATCH` if `AUX` drops unexpectedly after closing; move `U6` trigger to `Ā`; draw a full KiCad schematic and simulate in LTspice/SPICE before ordering parts; size `F1` to 30 A (or rely on the SE-1000-24 CC limit + branch fuses, and verify fuse clearing on the battery pack where short-circuit current is > 200 A).
   * **Confidence:** 95%

3. **C1 tie-rod joints will lose essentially 100% of preload to PETG creep, and the 4 mm 7075-T6 crank thread strength is overestimated**
   * **Severity:** Critical
   * **Evidence:** `docs/rnd/joints.md` (*Hardware*, *C1*). Yaw tie: 6 × M3×40 (12.9) at 1.0 kN → **9.5 MPa** continuous compression on printed PETG; pitch tie: 4 × M4×78.8 (10.9) at 1.5 kN → **8.8 MPa** continuous compression; both assume "Preload loss by creep × 0.75 (ESTIMATE)". Pitch studs thread **4 mm** through the 7075-T6 crank, credited with **11.6 kN** strip strength against **6.14 kN** stall load.
   * **Why it matters physically:** Steel bolts are far stiffer than printed PETG. At 1.0 kN over ~32–40 mm clamped length, an M3 bolt ($A_s = 5.03\text{ mm}^2$) stretches only **30–38 µm**; an M4 stud ($A_s = 8.78\text{ mm}^2$) at 1.5 kN over ~70 mm stretches **57 µm**. Under **8.8–9.5 MPa** static compression—especially near warm motors at 40–50 °C—FDM PETG typically experiences **0.2%–1.0% creep strain** over days to weeks, shortening a 40–75 mm tube by **80–750 µm**. That wipes out 100% of bolt preload (not 25%). Once unpreloaded, both joints gap under ordinary service loads, chewing up the printed hex/double-D torque fits and putting the M3/M4 bolts into cyclic bending fatigue. In addition, 4 mm tapped M4 (1.0D) minus thread chamfers leaves ~3.0–3.3 mm of effective engagement (~7–8 kN strip in 7075-T6, not 11.6 kN), giving ~1.15–1.3× margin at stall rather than 1.9×.
   * **Concrete fix or test:** Put **stacked Belleville (disc) spring washers** (e.g., DIN 2093, ≥ 0.4–0.6 mm elastic travel at 1.0–1.5 kN) under every tie-rod nut/head so 0.2 mm of PETG creep drops preload by < 30% instead of 100%, OR run the tie-rods through **internal aluminium/steel crush sleeves** (or switch the short pitch/yaw tubes to machined 6061 tubes bonded/keyed to printed pulleys). Thicken the 7075 crank to ≥ 6.5 mm (1.6D engagement) or use a through-nut/flanged boss. Run the 40 °C creep coupon for 168 h *with torque-to-re-tighten / bolt-tension measurement* before committing to un-sprung polymer clamping.
   * **Confidence:** 90%

4. **Severe tip-over instability (10/16 A10 cases fail up to −102 mm), and D18 anti-tip casters physically conflict with R4 thresholds and R5 doors**
   * **Severity:** Critical
   * **Evidence:** `docs/rnd/SCORECARD.md` (A10: worst ZMP **−102 mm**, only **6/16 cases ≥ 30 mm**; mass roll-up: base 6.31 kg + battery 1.10 kg = 7.41 kg vs upper core 8.50 kg + two arms 9.36 kg = **17.86 kg**, i.e., **71% of the 25.26 kg robot** is above the base); `rnd/params.py` (`wheel_radius_pos = 0.21` → 148 mm inscribed diamond radius; `clearance = 0.025`); `docs/RND_PLAN.md` (D2b: cross 15 mm thresholds at **45° heading**; R5: **70 cm doors** vs Ø50 cm base; D18: spring-loaded ball transfer casters, **5 N preload, 5 mm stroke**).
   * **Why it matters physically:** A 1.29 m tall robot with 71% of its mass high up on a 148 mm support radius is intrinsically top-heavy; adding a 9.1 kg (20 lb) laundry basket or a 30 N household bump guarantees tip-over outside narrow static poses. The D18 anti-tip ball-transfer units cannot solve this without breaking R4: if mounted 5 mm above the floor, they snag on 15 mm thresholds and drag in medium-pile carpet; if mounted 18 mm up to clear thresholds, the base must pitch **4.5°** before touchdown, shifting the 18 kg upper CG outward by **~70 mm** and dynamically slamming the tiny ball transfer into the floor. Similarly, approaching a 15 mm doorway threshold at a mandatory 45° heading (`D2b`) while leaving only 10 cm clearance per side in a 70 cm doorway is operationally brittle, and small passive omni rollers dig into medium-pile carpet and slip asymmetrically.

Simulate D18 caster ground clearance vs 15 mm threshold and carpet sinkage in MuJoCo—not just quasi-static flat-floor ZMP.
   * **Confidence:** 90%

5. **R17 pinch safety is hidden off the scorecard and relies on an unsafe software "person near" soft-limit plus a hazardous Category-0 free-fall drop**
   * **Severity:** Critical
   * **Evidence:** `docs/review/fable_review_2026-11-27.md` (*Minor*: `pinch.md` shows **62 nip fails and 31 exposed drive parts**; R17 omitted from `SCORECARD.md`); `docs/RND_PLAN.md` (D25: inboard shoulder-roll wedge **135 → 2 mm** over −15…+100° mitigated by a "software soft limit when people are near", roll ≥ +15°); `docs/rnd/electrical.md` (*Category-0 drop protection*: arm drops releasing **14–23 J**; gripper back-drives and drops payload; GIM8108-36 reflected inertia **2.17 kg·m²**).
   * **Why it matters physically:**
     * *Unacceptable software guarding:* A 103 N·m stall actuator (GIM8108-36) closing a 135 → 2 mm scissor wedge against the torso generates > 1,000 N of crushing force. Relying on non-safety-rated vision ("people are near") fails the instant a child approaches from behind or perception misses a hand—and worse, hitting e-stop removes torque so **gravity drops the arm right back through roll = +15° to −15°**, closing the exact pinch wedge it was supposed to prevent!
     * *Category-0 drop & impact inertia:* Dropping a 6 kg arm + 1–4.5 kg load onto whatever person/object triggered a stop (and dropping hot/breakable objects out of a back-drivable gripper) creates secondary injury. Meanwhile, the 36:1 inline roll's high reflected inertia (2.17 kg·m²) delivers a stiff transient impact spike before a 14–18 ms software collision observer can react.
   * **Concrete fix or test:** Put R17 pinch/exposed counts on `SCORECARD.md` as a hard gate; eliminate the shoulder-roll scissor mechanically by adding a **fixed hard stop at roll ≥ +10–15°** (accepting a small adduction trade) OR shaping the torso shoulder recess/conical shroud so the gap stays **≥ 25 mm** across the full mechanical range (or filled by a compliant closed-cell foam gusset); replace bare Category-0 free-fall on the full robot with either **hardware dynamic braking** (shorting motor phases via fail-safe NC relay/resistor bank on e-stop so the arm sinks slowly at < 0.2 m/s instead of free-falling) or a safety-rated SS1 delay relay.
   * **Confidence:** 95%

### The 5 Assumptions Most Likely Wrong

1. **"Printed PETG under 8.8–9.5 MPa static tie-rod compression retains 75% of bolt preload over its life" (`docs/rnd/joints.md`).** With only 30–57 µm of elastic bolt stretch on M3×40 / M4×78.8 fasteners, even 0.15%–0.3% viscoelastic creep (typical for PETG at 9 MPa and 40 °C) drops preload to **0%**, not 75%.
2. **"The GIM6010-8 / GIM8108-36 / GIM8108-8 all expose a clean, low-latency MIT impedance mode with flash-persistent torque limits and hardware CAN timeout" (`docs/M1_READINESS.md`, `docs/sw/sim2real_bench.md`).** Mixing three different SteadyWin driver generations (SDC104, SDC303, unknown 8108-8) on one arm makes it very likely at least one lacks MIT impedance mode, flash torque clamping, or a sub-50 ms CAN timeout—breaking the software safety argument.
3. **"A finned aluminium rear cap on the GIM6010-8 yields `h_o = 12 W/m²K` and restores 1.00× continuous torque inside R17 covers" (`docs/rnd/thermal.md`).** The rear face houses the driver PCB/connectors (blocking stator conduction), and natural convection inside a vented ≤ 8 mm consumer shroud is realistically **3–5 W/m²K**, leaving motors derated to ~0.65–0.75× rated.
4. **"An isotropic 1.8 GPa / 12 MPa PETG FEA model and an 80% gravity-compensation model yield 1.0 mm hand deflection" (`docs/rnd/fea.md`, `docs/rnd/stiffness.md`).** Printed infill + warm ambient drops effective modulus to ~1.0–1.3 GPa, cyclic Z-layer fatigue is ~5–7 MPa, and unmodeled V-band/carriage/15-arcmin-backlash compliance adds 5–8 mm of largely non-repeatable error that static gravity modeling cannot cancel.
5. **"A 500 Hz Python control loop on a Raspberry Pi 5 running 5 USB-CAN adapters, 3 cameras, and lidar stays below half a 25 ms (±20% = 20 ms) hardware watchdog" (`docs/rnd/electrical.md`).** Non-RT Python GC + USB3 camera/CAN interrupt bursts at ~73% CPU load will almost certainly produce > 20 ms stalls, causing frequent Category-0 drops unless the real-time loop moves to C/C++/Rust on an isolated `PREEMPT_RT` core.

### The 5 Strongest Aspects of the Design

1. **Disciplined, falsifiable parametric verification pipeline (`rnd/params.py` → CAD, MuJoCo twin, Monte Carlo, `assembly_seq.py`, `swept.py`, `pinch.py`).** Having automated geometry checks that catch bearing slide-on traps, internal clashes, swept clearances, and tool-driver access cones—and transparently tagging unverified values as `ESTIMATE`—is dramatically better than standard hobby/prototype robotics practice.
2. **Splitting M1 into a gated Step 1 / Step 2 purchase plan with explicit quantitative exit criteria (`docs/M1_BENCH_ARM.md`, `docs/M1_READINESS.md`).** Refusing to buy 4+ actuators before discovering the SDC104 CAN protocol, validating the safety board on a dummy load, and running a 2-hour hot/cold `sw/soak.py` sysid drift test saves thousands of dollars in sunk-cost mistakes.
3. **Architecture of the shoulder gravity balancer (`D4`, `A9`, `rnd/spring.py`) and lift ratchet pawl (`D5`, `A15`).** Canceling 40% of pitch self-weight with a real ISO 10243 die spring + 2:1 UHMWPE tackle cuts continuous motor heating substantially, and a fail-safe spring-applied steel ratchet pawl (`0 W` holding, safe during carry/e-stop) is the right mechanical solution for a vertical lift.
4. **Realistic bus-fault and sysid rehearsal framework (`docs/sw/sim2real_bench.md`, `sw/bus_sim.py`, `sw/sysid.py`).** Modeling jitter, packet loss, CRC errors, bus-off, and post-CRC payload corruption caught real latent bugs (stale queued replies, register-read watchdog starvation, stiction bias in static $K_t$ estimation) before touching hardware.
5. **Willingness to reverse flawed mechanical architectures based on evidence (`D13`, `D16`, `D17`, `D19`, `D24`, `C1`).** Abandoning print-in-place KX sockets, isolating wrist-roll loads onto a dedicated 2×61804 bearing spindle, moving small pulleys onto steel Ø12 shafts, bolting wheel hubs directly, and replacing pull-out M3 inserts with metal-threaded tie-rods shows strong engineering maturity—even though several of those recent fixes still need one final tightening pass to close cleanly.
