# Base stop, category 1: software half done, hardware / drive half needed (W14a)

**Why.** From docs/rnd/tip.md (*E-stop / snag braking*), the base tips if it brakes harder than about 1.0 m/s² with the R2d basket, 2.1 m/s² with 2.5 kg in the load box, and 1.7 m/s² with ≤ 1 kg and the arms straight out. The wheels can brake far harder than that (μ·g ≈ 5 m/s², ESTIMATE). So a base stop has to be a **controlled deceleration followed by torque-off** (stop category 1). A hard stop is not acceptable. The arm stays category 0 (electrical.md *Stop category*).

**Today's wiring defeats it.** In electrical.md the base branch (F 20 A → 4 wheel cartridges, CAN A) sits *downstream of K1*. An e-stop, a watchdog timeout or a supply trip removes base power at once, together with the arms. The clamp's 100 ms forced discharge then empties the bus. What happens next depends on the drive: it coasts if it floats its phases, or it brakes hard (tips) if it shorts them.

## Software half (sw/safety.py item 17, tested in sw/test_s1.py `test_base_stop`)
- Any latched fault starts a base ramp to 0 at `base_stop_decel()`. This is the smaller of the limit in force when the fault came (1.0 free, 0.5 with the casters not locked, 0.3 in load mode, 0.1 with a person near) and `BASE_STOP_DECEL`, tip.md's 30 mm-reserve column for ≤ 1 / ≤ 2.5 / ≤ 9.07 kg, and ∝ 1/payload past the table. **It is read from docs/rnd/tip.json at import** (`real.estop[*].a30`, each floored to 0.01 m/s²; today 1.17 / 1.50 / 0.52 from 1.176 / 1.502 / 0.526), no longer hand-copied: the copied tuple drifted above the column twice (W16a; W17d moved the 2.5 kg row 1.53 → 1.50). If the file is missing (a robot deployed without docs/), unreadable, lacks a row or holds an implausible value (outside 0.05–5 m/s²), the supervisor uses `BASE_STOP_DECEL_FALLBACK` = 1.00 / 1.30 / 0.45 m/s², 10–15 % under the W17d column; `BASE_STOP_DECEL_SRC` says which was used. No change in practice, even on the fallback: the limit in force (1.0 free, 0.5 released, 0.3 loaded) is ≤ either table. Worst case today: 0.8 m/s transit at 0.5 m/s² takes 1.6 s and 0.64 m. A basket carry at its locked cap (0.15 m/s since SW-T8; was 0.25, 0.3 before W15b) takes 0.50 s and 0.04 m; 2.5 kg at 0.20 m/s takes 0.67 s and 0.07 m.
- `base_torque_enabled()` / `base_heartbeat_ok()` stay True through the ramp. They go False once the command is at 0 and the measured speed (`limit_base(..., v_meas=)`, if odometry is passed) is under 0.01 m/s. In any case they go False BASE_STOP_GRACE (0.3 s) after the ramp's planned end ("base stop timeout").
- `heartbeat_ok()` (the arm channel) still drops at once.
- A collision hold alone ramps the base to 0 and keeps it powered.

## What the hardware / drive side must provide (electrical / base-drive track; not done here)
1. **A separate base power / enable channel.** It must not hang off the arm's K1. It needs its own watchdog line, toggled by `base_heartbeat_ok()`, and its own contactor or the drives' STO input.
2. **A hardware-timed SS1 ceiling.** The e-stop mushroom and a lost base heartbeat must remove base power or STO after a fixed delay, whatever the software does. Use a rated time-delay safety relay or a delayed-release safety output, set to ≥ the longest ramp + grace (1.6 + 0.3 s → **2.0 s**). It must never be a capacitor hold-up: electrical.md dropped that for the same reason. During the delay the wheel drives ramp.
3. **A drive-side ramp for when the software is gone** (process dead, Pi brown-out, CAN A silent). Each wheel cartridge needs its own quick-stop on CAN timeout and on its enable input, with a configured deceleration ≤ the planner's lowest loaded limit: **≤ 0.3 m/s² at the wheel rim**, or ≤ 0.5 m/s² if a payload-aware value can't be set. A drive whose only timeout behaviour is "disable" or "short the phases" fails this. Verify at bench bring-up, as for the GIM's CAN timeout (electrical.md: still unverified).
4. **No phase-short braking on disable or power loss**, or braking limited so a 0.8 m/s base never exceeds about 0.5 m/s². Whatever the drive does when STO or power is removed at the end of the ramp has to be gentle, because the base may still be creeping (slip, slope). It should coast; the caster locks (spring-applied, SW-T2 + SW-T8: engaged by then at ≤ 0.20 m/s (≤ 0.15 m/s above 6 kg), or by U20 removing VBUS_B) then hold the base.
5. **Regen on the base branch.** A ramp from 0.8 m/s with a 31 kg robot returns about 10 J. That is small, but the bus must still be up to absorb it, so the base branch needs the clamp (or its own) while K1 is open: the clamp's forced discharge on K1 opening must not empty the base side during the ramp.
6. **Odometry into the supervisor.** Wheel speed (CAN A, 250 Hz) goes to `limit_base(v_meas=...)` so torque-off waits for an actual stop instead of the timeout.

## Caster lock during the ramp (SW-T2 change from W15b, SW-T8 caps from W17d; sw/safety.py items 11 and 18, tested in sw/test_s1.py `test_caster_lock_at_speed` and `test_locked_cap`)
Engaging the D18b lock is harmless at any speed, but travelling locked at speed is not: a locked caster cannot be pushed up, so at 0.8 m/s a floor step becomes a snag (80–116 % of the tip energy, ≈ 3.6 kN on one foot; allowed 0.28 m/s; docs/rnd/safety_board_sim.md *Caster lock at speed*). So:
- **A lock waits for ≤ min(V_CASTER_LOCK = 0.25 m/s, the SW-T8 locked cap for the payload)**, i.e. 0.20 m/s up to 6 kg and 0.15 m/s up to 10 kg (V_CASTER_LOCK no longer binds; above 10 kg the cap is 0 and a lock waits for the base to stop, V_STOPPED). A forced lock (any latched fault, a person approaching or near, a payload past the release mode's limit) or the planner's `lock_casters()` above that speed puts the casters in state `locking`, counted "caster lock deferred". The solenoids stay energised (`caster_solenoid()` True) and `casters_locked()` stays False until the lock really engages. The speed cap drops to the locked cap (0 on a fault), and the base brakes on the fault ramp (item 17) or at the limit it was moving under (0.5 m/s² released), never past BASE_STOP_DECEL(payload).
- **It engages, with no re-seat pulse,** once the larger of the commanded and the measured speed (`limit_base(v_meas=)`; the command alone without odometry) is ≤ that speed. Unloaded from 0.8 m/s at 0.5 m/s² that is 1.20 s and 0.60 m into the ramp (1.40 s with odometry 0.2 s behind; W16a's 0.25 m/s gave 1.10 s). If odometry still reports more at that planned time + BASE_STOP_GRACE (1.50 s), or base torque goes off, it engages anyway ("caster lock timeout"). A payload step into a heavier band during the deferral re-plans that timeout for the lower speed (8 kg arriving mid-transit: 1.30 s, timeout 1.60 s). With a fault or a person about, no re-seat pulses run after it.
- **Threshold mode (≤ 0.12 m/s) and anything already slow lock at once,** as before.
- **The locked-travel cap follows the payload (SW-T8, W17d; sw/safety.py item 18, tested in sw/test_s1.py `test_locked_cap`).** `Supervisor.caster_locked_v_for(payload)` (a staticmethod, as `base_stop_decel_for`; table CASTER_LOCKED_V_BY): **≤ 6 kg 0.20 m/s, ≤ 10 kg 0.15 m/s, above 10 kg 0 (no travel with the casters locked)**. `base_speed_limit()` applies it whenever the casters are locked, locking or pulsing (it replaces the flat CASTER_LOCKED_V 0.25 m/s; CASTER_LOCKED_V is now the highest band, 0.20). Why: tip.md *Snag at the locked cap* re-ran W15b's snag on every travel state the supervisor allows (the basket in its real IK carry poses, pivot about the support polygon). At the flat 0.25 m/s only 2 of 23 states passed: every loaded or arms-out state is over the 440 N rod limit (½ the yield of a foot's two Ø8 rods), and the basket uses up to 43 % of the tip energy (limit 25 %). The lowest allowed speed in each band, rounded down to 0.05 m/s: ≤ 6 kg 0.202 → 0.20, 6–10 kg 0.168 → 0.15; at those caps every state passes. In W15b's own model the unloaded / 2.5 kg cases at 0.20 m/s give ≤ 240 N per foot.
- **A payload step into a heavier band while the base moves** brakes it to the new cap first (counted "locked cap lowered (payload)"), at the limit it was moving under and never past BASE_STOP_DECEL(new payload): 4 → 9.07 kg at 0.20 m/s brakes at 0.3 m/s² to 0.15 in 0.17 s; 0 → 8 kg at 1.0 m/s² capped to the table's 0.52; past 10 kg it brakes to 0. A lighter band raises the cap at once.
- **Threshold mode stays at 0.12 m/s** (THRESHOLD_SPEED is under every cap that allows travel; threshold releases stop at 2.5 kg). The D28 / SW-T7 basket rule is unchanged (> 2.5 kg moves only with the area clear). **Open:** the bench snag test in tip.md (foot force, pulse length, rod set) could move the ≤ 6 kg band back to 0.25 m/s; the basket band cannot go far past 0.15–0.20 m/s (its energy limit is 0.19).
- **The hardware locks without software.** The solenoids hang on VBUS_B (electrical.md *Base channel*), so they drop when U20 opens the base switch (2.0 s ± 10 % after any e-stop or board STOP; by then the deferred lock has engaged at 1.2–1.6 s) and at any power loss (spring-applied). Their gates have pull-downs, so a dead Pi cannot hold them released past U20. Residual (W15b): a total power loss in transit spring-locks the casters at up to 0.8 m/s.
